# PTJ-Inspired Strategy: Rates / FX Policy Divergence Macro Trade

> Research note for liquid macro markets. Policy divergence trades can trend hard, but they reverse violently when central-bank expectations shift.

## One-Sentence Thesis

When two major economies are moving in different policy and growth directions, express the divergence through the most liquid rates or FX instrument, with the stop tied to the macro pivot that would prove convergence instead.

## Cleanest Instruments

- Major FX pairs where the policy spread is central to price: EUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USD, AUD/USD, USD/CAD.
- Government bond futures: Treasury, Bund, gilt, JGB futures.
- STIR futures or swaps only for desks with appropriate infrastructure and risk controls.

## Market Context

The best context combines:

1. Central-bank reaction functions are diverging.
2. Inflation and growth data reinforce the divergence.
3. Rate differentials or yield curves are moving in the same direction as FX.
4. Positioning is not already one-way crowded, or there is a clear squeeze catalyst.
5. Liquidity is sufficient around the chosen contract and tenor.

## Setup

1. Choose whether the cleaner expression is FX or rates.
2. Identify the data/event sequence: CPI, payrolls, central-bank meetings, fiscal announcements, bond auctions.
3. Define the confirmation level in price and the macro data point that would invalidate the view.
4. Check cross-asset agreement: currency, front-end rates, long-end yields, equities, and commodities.

## Entry

- Enter after the policy divergence is confirmed by price, not before the event just because the narrative is attractive.
- Use a smaller probe ahead of event risk only if the stop is defined and gap risk is acceptable.
- Add when subsequent data confirms and the market makes a fresh trend high/low.

## Exit / De-Risk Plan

- Exit if the central-bank path reprices against the trade.
- Reduce before binary policy events unless the position is already paid for and protected.
- Trail the stop behind policy-relevant swing levels or moving-average support/resistance.
- Take profit when the market prices an extreme path and the next marginal data point no longer helps the thesis.

## Risk Point

- Invalidation is a repricing of the expected policy path, not just a small adverse tick.
- Define maximum loss in currency and as portfolio heat because FX/rates positions can overlap with equity and commodity risk.
- Avoid leverage that forces liquidation during normal event volatility.

## Example Template

- Context: U.S. data remains firm while Europe weakens; Fed pricing stays higher-for-longer while ECB pricing shifts dovish.
- Setup: EUR/USD breaks below a multi-week range as 2-year U.S.-Germany spread widens.
- Entry: Short EUR/USD on a failed retest of the broken range floor.
- Stop: Close back above the failed-retest high or a data release that narrows the policy spread materially.
- Add: Add only if the rate spread makes a new divergence high and EUR/USD confirms with a new low.
- Exit: Cover partials ahead of FOMC/ECB if the event could reverse the pricing.

## Failure Modes

- The central bank pushes back against market pricing.
- Growth surprise changes the relative policy path.
- Positioning is crowded and the trade squeezes on a minor catalyst.
- Political or intervention risk dominates the rate signal.

## Sources / Further Reading

- Bank for International Settlements papers and statistics on FX turnover and global liquidity.
- Central-bank policy statements, minutes, and dot plots / projections.
- CME Group contract specs and education on FX and Treasury futures.
- Jack D. Schwager, *Market Wizards*, macro trading interviews including Paul Tudor Jones.
