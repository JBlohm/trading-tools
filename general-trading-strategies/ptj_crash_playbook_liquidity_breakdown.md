# PTJ-Inspired Strategy: Crash Playbook / Liquidity Breakdown

> Defensive-first research note. Shorting crashes is dangerous: liquidity disappears, rallies are violent, borrow/derivative risk rises, and policy intervention can reverse the tape. Define the stop before entry.

## One-Sentence Thesis

When liquidity, breadth, credit, and price action all deteriorate, express downside through the most liquid index instrument with tight risk and fast de-risking rules.

## Cleanest Instruments

- Equity index futures or the most liquid index ETFs.
- Put spreads on liquid indices when gap risk is high and premium is acceptable.
- Avoid single-name shorts unless there is a separate idiosyncratic thesis; crashes are correlation events.

## Market Context

The setup is not just "market is down." It needs a deterioration cluster:

1. Major index below key moving averages or breaking a multi-month support shelf.
2. Breadth weakening: fewer stocks above moving averages, leaders failing, defensive sectors outperforming.
3. Credit stress: spreads widening, funding stress, volatility rising.
4. Macro pressure: tightening financial conditions, policy error risk, recession shock, inflation shock, or external stress.
5. Failed rallies: oversold bounces cannot reclaim prior breakdown levels.

## Setup

1. Mark the support level that defines the market's risk-on/risk-off line.
2. Wait for a decisive break or failed retest from below.
3. Confirm with volatility expansion and poor breadth.
4. Size smaller than normal because gaps and squeezes are part of the trade.

## Entry

- Initial short on a failed retest of broken support or a close below support with confirmation.
- Prefer adding on lower highs after profitable movement, not on the first emotional down day.
- If using options, prefer defined-risk put spreads when implied volatility is not already extreme.

## Exit / De-Risk Plan

- Cover part into panic extensions; crashes create air pockets but rebounds are brutal.
- Exit if the index recaptures the breakdown level, volatility compresses, or credit stress eases.
- Reduce before major policy events where intervention risk is high.
- Never turn a tactical crash short into a long-term opinion trade.

## Risk Point

- Primary invalidation: close back above the failed-retest high / broken support.
- Secondary invalidation: policy backstop or liquidity injection that changes the regime.
- Maximum loss must include overnight gap risk. If the gap risk is unacceptable, use options or do not trade.

## Example Template

- Context: Index loses 200-day moving average, credit spreads widen, volatility term structure inverts, rallies fail.
- Setup: S&P 500 futures break a 3-month support shelf, then retest it from below and fail.
- Entry: Short one half-unit on the failed retest.
- Stop: Close above the retest high.
- Add: Add only after a lower low and a lower high, with first unit stop reduced.
- Exit: Cover one third into a 3R extension or if a policy rescue changes liquidity conditions.

## Failure Modes

- Central-bank or fiscal intervention reverses the breakdown.
- Short squeeze from crowded positioning.
- Break happens on illiquid holiday trading and reverses immediately.
- Options bought after volatility explodes have poor reward/risk.
